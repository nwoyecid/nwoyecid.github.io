    <h1>Biography</h1>

    <p>
      <strong>Chinedu Innocent Nwoye (CID), Ph.D.</strong> is an AI Scientist at <a href="https://www.intuitive.com" target="_blank">Intuitive</a>, California, where he develops AI models for next-generation surgical robots and contributes to research on vision foundation models and vision-language models for surgery. He is a lead inventor on multiple patent applications and co-author of multi-institution studies on robot perception, foundation models, autonomous systems, and surgical AI. He is also the Co-Founder and Chief AI Officer of BetaVoid, building AI systems that automate prototyping and engineering calculations for automotive design.
    </p>
    <p>
      Previously, he was an AI Scientific Researcher at <a href="http://icube.unistra.fr/en/">ICube laboratory</a>, France, where he began in February 2018 with research focus on developing new deep learning applications in healthcare. He was also a post-doctoral research fellow at <a href="https://camma.unistra.fr/">CAMMA</a> <a href="https://www.ihu-strasbourg.eu/institut/presentation/">IHU Strasbourg</a>, where he led pioneering efforts in Surgical Generative AI.
    </p>
    <p>
      His research interests include Deep Learning, Computer Vision, Machine Learning and their applications in surgical assistive intervention and human-machine cooperation during surgery. His research objectives point towards the future generation intelligent operating room (OR). He is the principal organizer of the MICCAI Endoscopic Vision Biomedical Challenge on CholecTriplet, a groundbreaking initiative that models surgical activities through the lens of <em>surgical action triplet</em>, a representation of (tool-action-anatomy) relationships innovated by his PhD thesis.
    </p>
    <p>
      In addition to his research contributions, Dr. Nwoye has served as an Area Chair for <a href="https://sites.google.com/view/ipcai2024/organization" target="_blank">IPCAI 2024, 2025, 2027</a> and is a co-organizer of the <a href="https://sites.google.com/view/lm-surg/organization" target="_blank">NeurIPS workshop on Large Models Meet Surgical Data Science</a>. He also served as an adjunct lecturer (Enseignant vacataire) at the <a href="https://www.unistra.fr/index.php?id=english">University of Strasbourg</a>, where he shared his expertise and passion for advancing the field of surgical AI.
    </p>
    <p>
      He is the founder of the <em>surgical action triplet</em> research direction, a representation of instrument-action-anatomy relationships that has become a core paradigm in surgical AI. His models (Tripnet, Rendezvous) and benchmarks (CholecT45, CholecT50, CholecTrack20) are widely used in the community. His weakly supervised tracking models cut annotation cost by ~73% and now ship in the <a href="https://developer.nvidia.com/holoscan-sdk" target="_blank">NVIDIA Holoscan SDK</a>.
    </p>

    <h2>Education</h2>
    <p>
      He earned a <strong>Ph.D. in Computer Vision, Deep Learning, and Medical Robotics</strong> from the <a href="https://www.unistra.fr/index.php?id=english">University of Strasbourg</a>, France. His thesis, which won the <a href="http://ed.math-spi.unistra.fr/doctorat/prix-de-these/">MSII doctoral thesis prize 2022</a>, focused on the use of deep learning techniques for the recognition and detection of surgical tools and fine-grained activities in endoscopic videos. This thesis was supervised by <a href="http://camma.u-strasbg.fr/npadoy">Prof. Nicolas Padoy</a> and <a href="https://www.chru-strasbourg.fr/praticien/mutter-didier/">Prof. Didier Mutter</a>, examined and defended before <a href="https://campar.in.tum.de/Main/NassirNavabCv">Prof. Nassir Navab</a> (TU Munich), <a href="https://www.nct-dresden.de/forschung/departments-and-groups/department-for-translational-surgical-oncology.html">Prof. Stefanie Speidel</a> (NCT Dresden), and <a href="https://www.artorg.unibe.ch/research/aimi/group_members/persons/prof_dr_sznitman_raphael/index_eng.html">Prof. Raphael Snitzmann</a> (University of Bern).
    </p>
    <p>
      In 2017, he was among the pioneer students of the <a href="https://cmmrs.mpi-sws.org/">CMMRS</a> pre-doctoral program at Saarbr&uuml;cken, Germany co-organized by Cornell University, University of Maryland, and Max-Planck Institute for Software Systems.
    </p>

Prof. Sasan Mahmood</a> and <a href="https://www.ecs.soton.ac.uk/people/jsh2">Prof. Jonathan Hare</a>.

    </p>

    <h2>Flagship Research</h2>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Synthetic Surgical Data Generation (SurgGenAI)</strong>: Led a team developing diffusion-based models for text-to-image and joint image-mask generation of photorealistic surgical data at scale.</li>
      <li><strong>Surgical Tool Tracking (ANR DeepSurg)</strong>: Built weakly supervised models that cut annotation cost by ~73%, created CholecTrack20 and SurgiTrack, and integrated them into the NVIDIA Holoscan SDK.</li>
      <li><strong>Surgical Activity Modeling (CONDOR)</strong>: Built CholecT40/T45/T50 benchmarks and attention-based models for surgical action triplet recognition, and authored the ivtmetrics Python library.</li>
    </ul>
